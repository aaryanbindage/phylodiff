    How to use Phylodiff

    Phylodiff is a tree of life where every group has a 3D model. Pick any two and it tells you, in plain language, how they differ in size, surface material and colour. The tree is the NCBI taxonomy, the same one LifeMap draws.

    Try a comparison

    In three steps

    1. Find two things. Type a name into the search box above the tree (common or Latin names both work), or open groups by clicking the squares. A name you click goes into the highlighted slot, A or B, and then the highlight moves to the other slot.
    2. Look at them. The viewer shows A on the left and B on the right (Split). Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. Switch to Overlay to put B on top of A.
    3. Read the summary. The panel under the viewer lists what changed. Hover any row to light up that part on the models.

    Reading the summary

    • Size. Height, length and mass first, then the parts that grew or shrank the most. Bars pointing left mean A is larger, bars pointing right mean B is larger.
    • Surface material. Parts whose covering differs, such as skin to fur, scales to feathers or shell to gel.
    • Colour. The average colour of each body, then the parts whose colour changed most.
    • Parts. Parts that exist only on one side, such as a trunk, a mane or wings.

    Colours in the 3D view

    Amber: the part exists in both but differs in size, surface, colour or position.
    Red: only in A. In Overlay, A is drawn as a red wireframe.
    Green: only in B.
    Grey (Overlay only): parts that match.
    Turn off “Tint” to see natural colours in side-by-side view.

    About the data and the models

    • Tree. groups from the NCBI taxonomy, the same one LifeMap draws: every named group and species, down through genera to species and subspecies. Groups load from the server when you open them, so the whole tree is reachable. Placeholder entries such as “Genus sp.”, environmental samples and strain-level records are left out.
    • Zooming in. With “Auto-expand” on, small groups on screen open by themselves once you zoom in, so you see every member. Larger groups show a square: click it to open, and click “more” at the end of a long list to load the rest.
    • 18 hand-built models (elephant, mammoth, wolf, dog, human, chimpanzee, lion, cat, bear, horse, zebra, giraffe, okapi, great white shark, Nile crocodile, T. rex, ostrich, bald eagle) use measured proportions and masses.
    • Everything else is generated from the body plan of its group (mammal, bird, insect, tree, mushroom, bacterium, virus and about 30 more). Shapes are plausible but sizes and colours are illustrative, picked at random within a realistic range for the group. Do not use them as measurements.
    • No dates. NCBI does not give divergence times, so the tree shows branching order only.
